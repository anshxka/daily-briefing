Novadoz Pharmaceuticals Settles EEOC Charge Over Unequal Remote Work Policies

A New Jersey drug manufacturer has agreed to pay $116,666 to settle an EEOC discrimination charge brought by an American employee. The employee alleged that Novadoz Pharmaceuticals permitted Indian national employees to work remotely from India for personal reasons but denied her the same privilege, forcing her to take leave and ultimately pushing her out of her job in retaliation.

The EEOC found reasonable cause that the company violated Title VII of the 1964 Civil Rights Act and the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). Rather than proceeding to litigation, the dispute was resolved through pre-litigation conciliation. Novadoz and MSN Pharmaceuticals LLC agreed to revise their anti-discrimination and reasonable accommodation policies, implement new complaint procedures, conduct training, and submit to a two-year reporting period with the EEOC.

Arlean Nieto, acting director of EEOC's New York district, emphasized that employment decisions regarding remote work cannot be based on race or national origin. Nieto also highlighted that forcing an employee with a disability to take leave is unlawful when an alternative reasonable accommodation exists that would allow them to continue working.

While remote work has increasingly been recognized as a reasonable accommodation under the ADA since the COVID-19 pandemic, employers face legal risks when enforcing inconsistent remote work policies or applying blanket bans. Organizations must ensure that telework policies and accommodations are administered equitably without regard to protected characteristics.

Why it matters: This settlement serves as a critical warning for organizations to apply remote work and accommodation policies equitably across all employee groups. HR leaders should audit current telework guidelines to ensure compliance with Title VII and the ADA, avoiding discriminatory practices in location flexibility.
