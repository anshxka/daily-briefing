OpenAI Begins Watermarking ChatGPT Text in the European Union

OpenAI is rolling out an invisible watermark for text generated by ChatGPT and Codex within the European Union. This move is designed to comply with the EU AI Act's transparency rules, which took effect on August 2 and require AI companies to clearly mark AI-generated content so other systems can identify it.

The watermark functions by subtly shaping the model's word choices to leave an invisible pattern that detectors can spot, traveling with the text when copied and pasted. OpenAI's technical method, called textGrain, was developed with researchers from the University of Pennsylvania and Yale and relies on a secret key to sort next-word predictions.

However, tests show the watermark can be altered or removed by editing. Replacing 10% of the words with synonyms dropped detection accuracy from 92% to 66%, and short passages or translations remain difficult to detect, prompting OpenAI to limit initial detector access to approved researchers.

Why it matters: HR and business professionals must recognize that AI text watermarking is imperfect and easily disrupted by human editing. Relying solely on automated detection tools to verify original human work or compliance will remain unreliable.

HackerRank Launches AI Interviewer Chakra for Developer Hiring

HackerRank is making its AI interviewer, Chakra, generally available on Monday following a six-month beta period. During testing, the AI agent conducted over 500,000 interviews with companies like Snowflake, Snorkel, and Capgemini, as well as internal tests at HackerRank.

Chakra is designed to look more like a real-world job than a traditional coding test by giving candidates a task involving a real-world code repository and an AI assistant canvas. As the candidate works, Chakra observes them, asks follow-up questions about their approach, and evaluates critical thinking, judgment, and "AI fluency."

According to HackerRank CEO Vivek Ravisankar, Chakra combines what was previously a three-round process—a recruiter screen, take-home assessment, and engineering interview—into a single workflow. The tool aims to assess the thinking and judgment behind a candidate's work rather than just the final output.

Why it matters: This technology shifts technical screening toward evaluating critical thinking, problem-solving judgment, and AI fluency in real time. HR professionals can use these tools to streamline multi-step technical screening into a more efficient, realistic evaluation process.

UK Raises Illegal Work Fines to £60,000 and Tightens Right-to-Work Rules

The UK has implemented a significantly stricter statutory code of practice to prevent illegal working, updating its Right to Work scheme for employers. Under the new rules, employers face steep fines of £45,000 per illegal worker for a first breach, which increases to £60,000 for any repeat breach occurring within a three-year period.

The updated regulations extend liability to businesses operating through subcontractors and online work platforms. Online matching services now face rigorous requirements to verify worker identities, including digital re-verification through registered providers to combat account sharing and unauthorized substitutions. Additionally, employers hiring international students must meticulously document and retain academic term and vacation dates to maintain their statutory excuse against penalties.

The changes arrive as part of a broader crackdown affecting gig workers, international students, and tech firms utilizing outsourcing chains. Failure to maintain compliance and proper records across subcontracting networks can result in severe financial consequences for organizations operating in the UK.

What comes next involves heightened enforcement by the Home Office, pushing platforms and employers to rapidly adopt digital verification services and tighten administrative controls over student work hours and subcontractor vetting.

Why it matters: Organizations must immediately audit their compliance processes, subcontractor networks, and digital onboarding platforms to protect against massive financial penalties. HR leaders need to ensure robust record-keeping for student hours and verify that third-party vendors strictly adhere to the updated right-to-work mandates.

Transgender Former EEOC Employee May Proceed With Bias Lawsuit, Judge Rules

A federal magistrate judge has ruled that a transgender former employee of the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission may proceed with his lawsuit alleging discrimination and a hostile work environment. The plaintiff, who served as the agency's director of information governance and strategy, resigned in June 2025 following a series of institutional policy shifts under the leadership of Chair Andrea Lucas.

The lawsuit highlights policy changes enacted after Lucas was appointed acting chair, which included rolling back previous Biden administration initiatives regarding gender identity. According to the complaint, these actions involved targeting transgender employees, removing LGBTQ+ references from outreach materials, disbanding the internal LGBTQ+ employee resource group, and revoking internal nondiscrimination policies based on gender identity. The EEOC moved to dismiss the claims, arguing the allegations were merely generalized grievances stemming from lawful policy shifts.

However, U.S. Magistrate Judge Thomas Hixon sided with the plaintiff, holding that he has standing and has plausibly alleged sex-based harassment and a hostile work environment.

The case will now proceed further through the federal court system, potentially setting important legal precedents regarding agency policy changes and workplace discrimination claims based on gender identity.
