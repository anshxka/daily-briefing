New House Bill Proposes Fines Up to $250,000 for H-1B Visa Violations

A new bill introduced in the U.S. House of Representatives aims to significantly increase financial penalties and ban durations for H-1B visa program violations. The Trump administration has increasingly focused its enforcement agenda on legal immigration and foreign-worker hiring practices.

Representative House members introduced the H-1B Visa Fraud Crackdown Act, which seeks to amend the Immigration and Nationality Act. Under the proposed legislation, the maximum fine for serious H-1B violations would jump from $35,000 to $250,000, while a separate category of violations would see fines rise from $5,000 to $100,000. Additionally, the minimum period barring violators from participating in immigration programs would increase from three years to at least 10 years for serious infractions, and from two to at least five years for the other category. The bill also targets immigration-related document fraud.

The proposal has been referred to the House Judiciary Committee and must pass both the House and Senate before becoming law.
