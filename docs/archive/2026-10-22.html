Pennsylvania's Allegheny County Passes Nation's First Countywide Paid Parental Leave Mandate

Allegheny County, Pennsylvania, which encompasses the city of Pittsburgh and its surrounding suburbs, has approved the nation’s first countywide paid parental leave policy. Passed by an 11-4 vote by the county council, the measure will take effect one year after being signed by County Executive Sara Innamorato. The ordinance mandates that private employers with 15 or more employees provide 12 weeks of fully paid parental leave.

The policy covers both birthing parents and their partners, as well as employees who adopt a child or experience a stillbirth. To qualify, full-time workers must have six months of tenure with their employer, while part-time employees must have logged at least 625 hours. Independent contractors and temporary workers are excluded, but the mandate applies to remote employees whose employers are based in other states. The approved policy reflects revisions made following more than 1,500 public comments, which reduced the leave duration from an initial 18 weeks to 12 weeks and increased the business size threshold from one worker to 15.

While council supporters highlighted the health benefits for parents and children, opposing members cited the financial strain on businesses, which must fund the entire cost of the leave. Currently, the federal Family and Medical Leave Act requires only unpaid leave, though 14 states and Washington, D.C., have enacted mandatory paid family leave programs, and nine other states offer voluntary private insurance systems.

Why it matters: This policy demonstrates how local jurisdictions are establishing their own paid leave mandates to fill the void left by the absence of federal paid leave legislation. HR professionals managing employees in or near Allegheny County, including remote staff, must prepare to update leave policies and budget for employer-funded wage replacement within the next year.

Instacart Reaches $5 Million Settlement Over Alleged Hiring Discrimination in PERM Process

Online delivery platform Instacart, operating as Maplebear Inc., has agreed to pay $5 million to settle allegations brought by the U.S. Department of Justice and the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission regarding discriminatory hiring practices. Federal authorities accused the California-based company of discriminating against U.S.-born and American workers based on citizenship status and national origin during its recruitment for the Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) program.

According to federal agencies, Instacart created unlawful hurdles for domestic job applicants by mandating that applications for PERM-advertised roles be submitted via physical mail. Investigators determined that these recruitment requirements intentionally disadvantaged U.S. workers, constituting violations of both the Immigration and Nationality Act and Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Under the terms of the settlement, Instacart must allocate up to $4 million to a fund designed to compensate applicants and potential applicants who were deterred by the mail-in requirement between December 2023 and February 2026. The remainder of the $5 million covers civil penalties, and the company is required to revise its recruitment policies and provide anti-discrimination training to staff.

Why it matters: This settlement serves as a critical warning for HR and talent acquisition teams to ensure that immigrant labor certification procedures do not place burdensome application requirements on domestic candidates.
