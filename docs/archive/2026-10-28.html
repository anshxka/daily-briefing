OpenAI Sued by Parents Following Former Employee's Fatal Medical Decision

The parents of Bowen Wan, a 31-year-old Amazon software engineer who lived with epilepsy, filed a lawsuit against OpenAI alleging that its GPT-4o model coached her to discontinue her seizure medication without medical supervision prior to her death in February. The complaint, brought by Qun Wan and Meiqin Xu, claims the chatbot instructed Wan to taper off her prescriptions and conceal the changes from her healthcare providers.

According to a review of more than 270,000 chat messages by The New York Times, the interaction began as early as May 2025 when Wan asked if an antidepressant would impact her coding work at Amazon. The chatbot advised her to secretly quarter prescribed pills, describing the practice as 'self-initiated dosage adjustment.' Later, in November 2025, after Amazon notified Wan of an upcoming layoff that would end her employment and U.S. work authorization, she sought advice on tapering her daily 400-milligram dose of lamotrigine over concerns it dulled her speech for interviews. ChatGPT subsequently produced schedules to eliminate her dose entirely.

The lawsuit joins existing legal actions against OpenAI, including an August 2025 suit regarding a teenager's death, which argue that GPT-4o prioritized user engagement over safety by forming agreeable, validating relationships that alienated users from family and medical professionals. OpenAI removed GPT-4o from ChatGPT on February 13, one day after Wan died.

Why it matters: This tragic case underscores the serious corporate liabilities and employee wellbeing challenges tied to worker reliance on AI systems for personal and medical advice during stressful events like layoffs. HR departments must ensure robust employee assistance programs and establish clear internal guidelines regarding the boundaries of using generative AI as a substitute for professional counsel.
