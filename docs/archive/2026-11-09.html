Elvis and Eminem Rights Holders Sue AI Music Platform Suno Over Voice Clones

AI music generation service Suno is facing a new pair of lawsuits brought by rights holders of Elvis Presley's music and the publisher of major tracks by Eminem. The lawsuits accuse Suno of illegally creating audio and lyrics that mimic the artists. Attorneys representing Presley's estate claim that the company violated Tennessee's Ensuring Likeness Voice and Image Security (ELVIS) Act, a statute that explicitly prohibits AI developers from cloning a vocalist's voice.

The complaints argue that Suno has mechanically encoded musicians' creative work and identities without permission to build a profitable commercial product. These filings closely mirror a lawsuit brought against Suno in September by Jason Isbell and other artists over unauthorized imitations, while adding to other active legal actions filed by major labels and publishers, including Sony, Universal Music Group, and Reservoir Media.

Suno spokesperson Cat McCormack stated that the platform is designed to assist creators in producing original music rather than trading on an individual's name or likeness. McCormack explained that the service bans unauthorized uses of artist work, prohibits text prompts that include specific artist names or song titles, and uses third-party screening tools to monitor uploaded lyrics and audio.

Why it matters: This expanding litigation demonstrates the serious legal and intellectual property liabilities companies encounter when utilizing generative AI trained on proprietary or unauthorized media. HR and compliance managers should establish clear policies to ensure employees do not use unauthorized generative platforms for public marketing, corporate creative work, or external communications.
