Federal Appeals Court Clarifies Rules on Employee PTO Preservation During Disability Leave

Employers frequently navigate complex interactions between accrued paid time off (PTO) and leave granted as a disability accommodation. In a recent case, Kingsolver v. U.S. Attorney General, the 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals held that the Rehabilitation Act—the public-sector counterpart to the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)—does not give employees an explicit right to unpaid leave when they prefer to save accrued PTO for future needs.

Neither the ADA nor its accompanying regulations explicitly entitle employees to preserve PTO, nor do they explicitly allow employers to mandate its exhaustion in the manner Family and Medical Leave Act regulations do. While Equal Employment Opportunity Commission guidance notes employers should permit workers with disabilities to exhaust accrued paid leave before transitioning to unpaid leave, the 11th Circuit affirmed that federal law allows employers to select among any effective accommodation. Because offering paid leave was reasonable, the worker could not demand unpaid leave simply to preserve PTO.

To navigate these requests, legal experts advise HR professionals to examine existing company leave policies first. Most businesses currently maintain policies requiring accrued paid time off to run concurrently with statutory accommodations or disability leaves.

Why it matters: This ruling confirms that employers generally hold the right to determine reasonable accommodations, including requiring the concurrent use of PTO rather than granting unpaid leave. HR leaders should review their leave policies to ensure clear, consistent guidelines on whether accrued paid time off must be exhausted during accommodation leaves.
