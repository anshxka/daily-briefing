United Airlines Blocks Third-Party AI Agents from Booking and Managing Flights

United Airlines announced it is restricting access for autonomous AI agents seeking to book flights and manage reservations on behalf of users. The airline cited cybersecurity risks and potential violations of federal regulations as the primary drivers behind the policy update.

The decision comes as companies across the airline and banking sectors grapple with distinguishing real customers from automated systems. United warned that when users share their login credentials with third-party personal AI assistants, it creates severe security vulnerabilities. These practices can lead to leaked credentials, unintended purchases, unauthorized account changes, or the misuse of MileagePlus miles. Furthermore, the airline noted that AI tools could cause violations of federal aviation rules, such as mandatory human check-ins to certify that baggage is free of banned substances.

In response to these risks, United is updating its booking, ticketing, and distribution policies to prohibit unauthorized third-party AI agents directly on its digital platforms and indirectly through travel agencies and aggregators.
