Judge Orders OpenAI to Hand Over Hiring Records in Apple Lawsuit

A federal judge has ruled that Apple will gain expedited access to OpenAI's hiring materials and interview documents as part of an ongoing trade secrets lawsuit. Apple filed the suit against OpenAI in July, accusing the company of a pattern of theft regarding unannounced technologies, features, and products.

Under the court order, OpenAI must turn over interview checklists, guides, instructions, manuals, scripts, and technical presentations within five to fourteen days. The court also approved depositions for three former Apple employees now at OpenAI: Chang Liu, Alyssa Peng, and Chief Hardware Officer Tang Yew Tan. However, the judge denied Apple's broader requests for access to OpenAI's internal research and development in areas like metal finishes and power management.

The case stems from Apple's allegations that former employees downloaded confidential files on unannounced hardware products and coached others on what material to bring to interviews. The legal battle intensifies as OpenAI develops its own consumer hardware device, potentially positioning the company to rival the iPhone in the future.

Why it matters: This high-stakes legal battle serves as a stark reminder for HR and business leaders to rigorously enforce non-disclosure agreements and intellectual property protections during recruitment. Organizations hiring from competitors must ensure strict compliance to avoid costly trade secret litigation and reputational damage.
