Proxy Advisory Firms Urge Sebi Intervention Over Tata Steel and APL Apollo Deal

Three proxy advisory firms—IiAS, SES, and InGovern—have called on the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi) to intervene in the complex, multi-part transaction between Tata Steel Ltd and APL Apollo Tubes Ltd. The deal involves Tata Steel selling two units to APL Apollo for ₹28,000 crore funded through debt, followed by Tata Steel acquiring a controlling stake in the tubes manufacturer through promoter buyouts and an open offer, alongside an exclusive contract-manufacturing arrangement.

According to the advisory firms, the deal structure was engineered to bypass mandatory shareholder approval requirements for related-party transactions. Because Tata Steel was technically not a related party to APL Apollo on Sunday evening when the asset sale was decided, the companies avoided standard related-party rules. However, advisors argue this violates Sebi's "purpose and effect" test introduced in April 2023, which dictates that transactions should be assessed as a whole rather than separate announcements when they collectively benefit related parties.

As APL Apollo prepares to borrow heavily to finance the asset purchase, the market regulator is being urged to examine whether the arrangement amounts to a differential payment to investors and whether related-party regulations were effectively circumvented.

Why it matters: Corporate governance professionals must closely monitor regulatory interpretations of related-party transactions and deal-structuring compliance. This case serves as a critical reminder that regulators are increasingly looking at the holistic economic impact of commercial agreements rather than just technical legal separation.
