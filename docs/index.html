US Lawmakers Introduce Bill to Raise Key H-1B Exemption Salary Threshold to Nearly $140,000

Republican lawmakers in the United States have introduced the Protecting American Workers Through H-1B Modernization Act, a bill seeking to raise an H-1B salary exemption threshold to nearly $140,000 per year. Sponsored by Representative Glenn Grothman of Wisconsin and Senator Jon Husted of Ohio, the legislation aims to update a benchmark that has remained set at $60,000 since 1998.

The proposed $140,000 figure would not function as a mandatory minimum wage for every H-1B worker. Instead, it governs exemptions from extra hiring safeguards placed on "H-1B-dependent" employers, which are companies that maintain a high ratio of foreign professionals relative to their broader workforce. Currently, these employers can bypass requirements to recruit domestic talent and attest against displacing American workers if their H-1B hires earn at least $60,000 or hold a master's degree or higher.

Under the proposed measure, the fixed $60,000 threshold would be replaced by a formula tied to twice the Social Security Administration's National Average Wage Index, which currently equates to nearly $140,000 and would adjust over time with wage shifts. Grothman and Husted argued that the existing standard has lagged behind wage growth, allowing heavily dependent firms to bypass domestic worker protections.

Why it matters: If enacted, this legislation would make maintaining H-1B exemptions considerably more expensive for visa-dependent employers and require stricter recruitment compliance. HR and talent mobility leaders must monitor the bill to evaluate potential impacts on foreign talent budgets and domestic hiring attestations.
